    Maple runs the same way on one laptop and in a company: a proxy holds the accounts and talks to the models, and your tools talk to a proxy. The setups below differ only in how many proxies there are and how they are joined. Start with the first one that fits, and move to another later: nothing you set up is lost (see How to move between setups).

    In plain words #

    Answer three questions:

    1. Is it just you? Then use one laptop. Install it, sign in, work. Nothing on this page is needed, and nothing here shows up until you set it up.
    2. Does your company or team already run a Maple hub? Then join your laptop's proxy to it: sign in with your company account (about a minute), or paste a token the hub's admin gives you. Maple Harness keeps working exactly as before; it now also reaches the hub's models.
    3. Are you the one setting up the hub? Then pick by size:
      • one machine for a team: a team hub;
      • a hub per team, with one company hub above them: chained hubs;
      • a hub that must keep running through restarts and lost machines: several replicas.

    And for a CI job, a script or a container, see CI and containers: start the proxy in the job, and nothing needs setting.

    These combine: a company hub can run as several replicas, with single sign-on, a team hub chained below it, and laptops signed in to the team hub.

    Words used on these pages #

    WordWhat it means
    Proxymaple-proxy, the program that holds sign-ins and keys and talks to the models. Every Maple install has one
    HubA proxy that other machines or other proxies use. Nothing extra is installed: a hub is a proxy that listens on the network and asks every caller for a token
    Personal tokenAn access token that belongs to one person. Everything sent with it counts as that person. Your proxy gets one when it signs in with your company account; an admin can also make one for you, or you make one on the hub's My access page
    Chain tokenA token the hub's admin makes for one proxy that serves many people. It lets that proxy bring the hub's people down, send its usage up, and say who asked for each request
    Sign-in addressThe hub's single sign-on address, such as https://maple-admin.example.com, where people sign in with their company account. It is not the address of the hub's models
    Parent hubThe hub a proxy joined with a chain token (chain.parent). A hub you signed in to with your company account is never a parent

    1. One laptop #

    Pick this if you use Maple on your own. This is the setup everyone starts with, and most people stay with.

    What you run. maple-harness. It starts maple-proxy on the same machine when nothing is running, and you sign in to your accounts on the proxy's setup page (http://127.0.0.1:11539). Claude Code, OpenCode and other tools on the same machine can use the same proxy without a token (Use your models from other tools).

    What it guarantees.

    • The proxy listens only on this machine (127.0.0.1), and its setup page refuses to listen anywhere else.
    • Maple Harness checks, on every connection, that the proxy it talks to is your own, with a key only you can read (Verified and unverified proxies).
    • Your keys stay in your system keyring or secret store; config.yaml holds only references.
    • No people, teams, hubs or tokens appear anywhere until you set them up.

    What can go wrong, and how you see it.

    What happensHow you see itWhat to do
    The proxy is still the old release after an updateBefore a session: "Your proxy at … is an older release than this harness", with [r] restartPress r, or maple-harness proxy restart
    The proxy runs as another user, in a container, or with another settings folder"You're connecting to an unverified proxy at …"; tools ask before every runHow to fix it
    Another program holds the proxy's port"something else is answering on <address>, not your Maple proxy"Stop that program, or move the proxy's ports in server:
    An account is out of credits or rate limitedThe request fails with that reason, and Provider health on the setup page shows itAdd a fallback (Fallbacks)

    Step by step: Install, then Your first session and Sign in.

    2. Your laptop's proxy joined to a team hub #

    Pick this if your team or company runs a Maple hub and you want its models, under your own name, budget and model list.

    What you run. Nothing new. Your own proxy joins the hub, and Maple Harness keeps talking to your own proxy, as on one laptop. Every tool that uses your proxy (Maple Harness, Claude Code, OpenCode) then reaches the hub's models as hub/<model>, next to your own accounts if you keep any. There are two ways to join:

    Sign in with your company account (recommended)Paste a personal token from the hub's admin
    WhenThe hub has single sign-on with developer sign-in onAny hub, with or without single sign-on
    HowYour proxy's setup page (Hubs & chaining, Sign in with your company account), maple-proxy chain connect --sso <sign-in address>, or the shortcut maple-harness proxy connect --sso <sign-in address>Your proxy's setup page (Hubs & chaining, Connect this proxy to a parent hub, under Or paste a token from the hub's admin); or a provider of type: maple in config.yaml
    What you needThe hub's sign-in address and your company accountThe hub's model address (ending in /v1) and your token
    How long it lasts12 hours at a time, renewed by your proxy for up to 7 days, then you sign in againUntil it expires (30 days for one you made on My access, unless your admin chose another length; never for one an admin made, unless they chose a date) or is revoked
    The hub counts requests asYouYou

    maple-harness proxy connect --sso does not sign the harness in: it has your local proxy sign in, prints the link and the code, and the harness keeps using your local proxy. No token reaches the harness.

    With a pasted personal token, Test the connection says who the hub counts you as ("Connected as <you> at <hub>: your requests count as you"), and Save keeps it without asking. The page leaves Use the hub's people and teams here, and send usage up unticked and greyed out: a personal token uses the hub's models as you, and making the hub this proxy's parent needs a chain token (setup 4). A hub before 2.2.0 cannot say whose a token is: the test then passes with a note saying so.

    On the hub, the admin's People & teams shows each person's How they connect lines, with this way first:

    What it guarantees.

    • One place for your hub credential: your proxy. The harness never holds a hub sign-in.
    • Everything your proxy sends counts as you at the hub: your budget, your allowed models and accounts. Who on your machine used your proxy is not sent; that is why this is for your own laptop.
    • Your proxy keeps its own accounts, so you can fall back either way: your own account first and the hub when it runs out, or the hub first and a local model when the hub says no (Fallback across the chain).
    • A sign-in renews itself; nothing about your company account is kept on your laptop, only the hub's token for you.

    What can go wrong, and how you see it.

    What happensHow you see itWhat to do

    Step by step: Join your laptop to a team hub, and How developers sign in for the hub's side (My access).

    3. A team hub for many people #

    Pick this if several people should share accounts, and you want to see who used what, give each person or team their own accounts, or cap what each can spend.

    What you run. One maple-proxy on a machine your team can reach (a server, a container, or one replica on Kubernetes), listening on the network, with:

    • people and teams, each person with their own token (Teams);
    • optionally single sign-on, so admins sign in to the setup page with their company account and developers make their own tokens and sign their proxies in on My access (How developers sign in).

    Each teammate then joins their own proxy to it (setup 2). Tools without a Maple proxy of their own (Claude Code or OpenCode on a machine with no Maple install) use the hub's address with the person's token as their API key.

    What it guarantees.

    • Off this machine every request needs a token with the proxy scope, and the proxy will not start on the network until one exists. A request under a host name not in server.allowed_hosts is refused.
    • Every request made with a person's token counts as that person; their budget, model list and accounts apply.
    • The setup page stays on the hub's own machine. Through single sign-on, admins get a second, signed-in address that can do less (no keys, no providers, no machine details).

    What can go wrong, and how you see it.

    What happensHow you see itWhat to do
    No token with the proxy scope exists yetThe proxy does not start on a network address, and says whyMake one on People & teams first
    A teammate's tool uses a name the hub does not know403 from the hub's addressAdd the name to server.allowed_hosts
    A token is wrong, revoked or expired401, "a valid access token with the proxy scope is required"Make a new token for the person
    A budget is spent429, budget_exceededWait for the reset, or raise it
    Plain HTTP on a network others shareNothing visible: anyone on that network can read the tokensUse HTTPS, or a private network such as Tailscale or a VPN

    The full list of refusals is in When a request is refused.

    Step by step: Share Maple with a team, then Set up a team on the People & teams tab, and Single sign-on if you want it.

    4. Hubs chained to a company hub #

    Pick this if there is more than one hub: a hub per team or per office, with people defined once and every hub's usage in one place.

    What you run. A proxy per team, each joined to the hub above it with a chain token made by that hub's admin (Hubs & chaining, Let a proxy connect to this hub), pasted on the team proxy (Connect this proxy to a parent hub). Laptops join the team hub as in setup 2.

    What it guarantees.

    • People and teams are defined once, at the top, and handed down; a hub below may add its own and tighten an inherited budget or model list, never loosen it.
    • Usage goes up: the top hub reports on every proxy below it, each request counted once. Only counts and labels go up, never prompts, answers or files.
    • Who asked travels up with each request, and only a proxy holding a chain token may say who asked. A chain token belongs to the first proxy that uses it.
    • A revoked token stops working on every proxy below within seconds while the hubs answer.

    What can go wrong, and how you see it.

    What happensHow you see itWhat to do
    The parent hub cannot be reachedRequests without a fallback fail as unreachable; after chain.directory_grace (15 minutes) inherited tokens are refused, the proxy's own keep workingFix the network; usage waits and is sent later, once
    A proxy below stopped askingThe hub map marks it not heard lately after five minutesCheck that proxy
    The chain token is already used by another proxyTest the connection says it is bound to another proxyUnbind it on the hub, or make a new one
    A loop, or more than chain.max_hops proxies (4)Status 508Fix the chain
    A child tries to loosen an inherited ruleThe command refuses it, naming the inherited value; the People card offers only TightenAsk the hub above to change it

    Step by step: Chain proxies into hubs, and Chained hubs for a worked example with screenshots.

    5. Several replicas behind a load balancer #

    Pick this if a hub (setup 3 or 4) must keep answering through a restart, an upgrade or a lost machine. One replica is enough for most teams: the proxy is light and restarts in seconds.

    What you run. Copies of one proxy behind a load balancer, with storage.mode: central, one PostgreSQL and one Redis. Then choose where the settings live:

    Settings in PostgreSQL (recommended)Settings from a file (a ConfigMap)
    Settingstorage.settings: postgres (the default in central mode)storage.settings: file
    Change a settingOn any replica's setup page; every replica runs it within secondsEdit the file and roll the replicas out; read-only on the page
    Can replicas drift apart?No: one that cannot confirm it runs the current settings is not readyYes, for a while: Maple reports it, and can take the odd one out
    Samplesconfig/ha-shared (Kubernetes), compose.ha-shared.yaml (Docker)config/ha (Kubernetes)
    A good fit whenAdmins change settings on the pageEvery change goes through review and Git

    What it guarantees.

    • Any replica serves any request: no sticky sessions, rate limits and budgets counted across replicas, signed-in browsers known to every replica.
    • With settings in PostgreSQL, every replica runs the same configuration, and one that cannot confirm it is current leaves the load balancer (/readyz says not ready) until it is.
    • A replica that stops finishes its running requests first.

    What can go wrong, and how you see it.

    What happensHow you see itWhat to do
    PostgreSQL is away/readyz says not ready; requests of people with a budget get 503 (unless storage.budgets_when_down: open); settings cannot changeBring it back; waiting usage lines are written once
    Redis is away/readyz says not ready; the setup page answers 503; caches missBring it back; browsers sign in again
    Replicas run different settings (from a file)Data & storage, Settings per replica (in red, below); /healthz adds replicas disagree on configFinish the rollout; see Replicas that disagree
    Shared keys differ between replicasThat replica is not ready, and Data & storage says soGive every replica the same storage key
    The load balancer adds X-Forwarded-For but is not trustedOne client's wrong tokens get every client refused with 429 for a minuteList the balancer in server.trusted_proxies

    A proxy with replicas cannot keep a company-account sign-in to a hub above it: join it with a chain token.

    Step by step: Kubernetes: several replicas and Docker: several replicas.

    6. CI jobs and containers #

    Pick this if Maple Harness runs in a CI job, a script or a container, where nobody is at a terminal to answer.

    In plain words. A run that nobody can answer (--prompt, --rpc, serve, or no terminal) uses the proxy on the same machine only when it can check that the proxy is yours: started as the same user, with the same settings folder. If it cannot check, the run does not start; it exits with status 1 and says how to fix it. It never waits for an answer. So, from the simplest to the most work:

    1. Start the proxy in the job (recommended). Give the job its config.yaml and run maple-harness: it starts its own proxy when nothing answers, or you start maple-proxy serve yourself first, as the same user. The harness checks that proxy on every connection, and you set nothing.
    2. A proxy in a service container (or any proxy the job does not start): connect to it with maple-harness proxy connect and an access token kept in the job's secret store. A container on its own network always needs a token, so this is the way for it.
    3. A proxy on 127.0.0.1 that needs no token but is not yours (run by another user, with another settings folder, or a container on the host's network): set MAPLE_TRUST_UNCHECKED_PROXY=1 for the run, or harness.proxy.trust_unchecked: true in config.yaml.

    SettingNoneharness.proxy.url and harness.proxy.token, written by proxy connectThe variable, or harness.proxy.trust_unchecked: true
    How the harness knows the proxyIt proves it is yours, on every connectionIts address and its access tokenIt does not: you said to go on
    ToolsRun as your rules and --yolo sayRun as your rules and --yolo sayRun as your rules and --yolo say
    Access tokenNone neededSent to that address on every requestNever sent: a proxy that needs one refuses the requests

    GitHub Actions: start the proxy in the job #

    The harness starts the proxy from the same settings folder, so it is verified. The provider's key comes from the job's secrets through an environment variable; nothing secret is written in the file.

    jobs:
  agent:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v5
      - name: Install Maple Harness
        run: curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kinncj/maple-harness-dist/main/install.sh | bash
      - name: Settings for this job
        run: |
          mkdir -p ~/.config/maple-harness
          cat > ~/.config/maple-harness/config.yaml <<'EOF'
          version: 1
          server:
            openai_compat: 127.0.0.1:11535
            anthropic_compat: 127.0.0.1:11537
            webui: 127.0.0.1:11539
          providers:
            - id: models
              type: openai-compatible
              base_url: https://models.example.com/v1
              api_key: ${env:MODELS_API_KEY}
              models:
                - id: your-model
          model_aliases:
            coder-default: your-model
          EOF
      - name: Run the agent
        env:
          MODELS_API_KEY: ${{ secrets.MODELS_API_KEY }}
        run: maple-harness --yolo --prompt "Fix the failing test" --json-summary summary.json

    The same in any other CI, or a plain shell script:

    export MODELS_API_KEY=...            # from your CI's secret store
mkdir -p ~/.config/maple-harness     # then write config.yaml as above
maple-harness --yolo --prompt "Fix the failing test"

    To see the proxy's log, start it yourself first, as the same user, and wait until it answers:

    maple-proxy serve > proxy.log 2>&1 &
for i in $(seq 30); do curl -sf http://127.0.0.1:11535/v1/models > /dev/null && break; sleep 1; done
maple-harness --yolo --prompt "Fix the failing test"

    What it guarantees. The harness talks only to the proxy that proves it holds this settings folder's key, so tools run as your rules say. Keep the server: addresses in the file: without them the harness has no proxy address.

    GitHub Actions: a proxy in a service container #

    A proxy in a container listens beyond its own loopback, so it asks every caller for an access token (see Run the proxy in Docker); no image is published, so use your own build of the Docker sample. Keep the token's value in the job's secrets. The container is given the value, and the harness sends it with mh_ in front:

    jobs:
  agent:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    services:
      maple-proxy:
        image: registry.example.com/maple-proxy:2.2.0   # your build of the Docker sample
        ports:
          - 127.0.0.1:11535:11535
        env:
          MAPLE_SECRET_ACCESS_PROXY_TOKENS: ci=${{ secrets.MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN_VALUE }}
          # its providers and keys: see Run the proxy in Docker
    env:
      MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN: mh_${{ secrets.MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN_VALUE }}
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v5
      - name: Install Maple Harness
        run: curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kinncj/maple-harness-dist/main/install.sh | bash
      - name: Connect to the proxy container
        run: maple-harness proxy connect --token-env MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN http://127.0.0.1:11535
      - name: Run the agent
        run: maple-harness --yolo --prompt "Fix the failing test"

    The same in a shell script, with the token in MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN:

    maple-harness proxy connect --token-env MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN http://127.0.0.1:11535
maple-harness --yolo --prompt "Fix the failing test"

    proxy connect checks that the proxy takes the token, then writes harness.proxy.url and harness.proxy.token: ${env:MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN} into the job's config.yaml; the token itself is never written.

    A proxy on 127.0.0.1 with no token, that the harness cannot verify (one run as another user or with another settings folder, or a container with --network host):

    MAPLE_TRUST_UNCHECKED_PROXY=1 maple-harness --yolo --prompt "Fix the failing test"

    In GitHub Actions, put MAPLE_TRUST_UNCHECKED_PROXY: "1" in the step's env:.

    What it guarantees. With proxy connect, the harness trusts the proxy by its address and token, and never starts, stops or restarts it. With MAPLE_TRUST_UNCHECKED_PROXY=1, nothing secret is sent to the proxy: no access token, even one in harness.proxy.token. So a proxy that needs a token is connected with proxy connect, never trusted this way: a run trusted this way that meets one stops at its first refusal and says to connect to it.

    When a run does not start #

    A run that nobody can answer, on a proxy the harness cannot verify, ends at once with exit status 1 and these lines on standard error (nothing on standard output, and no --rpc events):

    You're connecting to an unverified proxy at 127.0.0.1:11535:
it runs as another identity: another user, another settings folder, or a container.
...
This session cannot ask you, so it did not start.

    Exit status 1 is also what other errors use; to tell this one apart in a script, look for the line This session cannot ask you, so it did not start.:

    maple-harness --prompt "Fix the failing test" 2> harness.err
status=$?
if [ "$status" -ne 0 ] && grep -q "so it did not start" harness.err; then
  echo "the proxy on this machine is not verified: see the CI section of Which setup do I need?" >&2
fi

    maple-harness proxy status says the same before a run: its check line names an unverified proxy.

    The technical view. "Verified" means the proxy answered a one-time challenge on that very connection with a signature only the holder of the settings folder's local-identity.key can make (see Verified and unverified proxies). The proxy the harness starts, or one you start as the same user with the same XDG_CONFIG_HOME, has that key. A container that does not share the folder, a proxy of another user, or one reached through a port forward does not. A token from harness.proxy.token is sent to a proxy on this machine only after it proved itself, so the 2.1 way of naming a token without harness.proxy.url needs proxy connect now.

    There are two ways to reach a proxy you do not start, and they are different things:

    Use a proxy or hub directly with a token (advanced)Trust a proxy on the same machine that cannot prove it is yours
    WhenA CI job or container that uses a proxy container or a team hub, with no proxy of its ownA proxy run as another user, or with another settings folder, on 127.0.0.1 with no token
    Settingharness.proxy.url and harness.proxy.token, written by maple-harness proxy connect <address>harness.proxy.trust_unchecked: true, or MAPLE_TRUST_UNCHECKED_PROXY=1 for one run
    What changesThe harness sends every request to that address with the token, and never starts, stops or restarts itTools run as your rules say instead of asking before every run
    What stays offDelegate to your local Claude; auth, savings and the quota meter run on the proxy's machineNothing secret is sent to that proxy, and Delegate to your local Claude stays off

    For a hub on another machine, the same command takes its address:

    maple-harness proxy connect --token-env MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN https://hub.example:11535

    The variable's name must contain TOKEN or KEY (so commands the model runs do not see it). Without --token-env, the command asks for the token without showing it, or reads it from standard input, and keeps it in the secret store. maple-harness proxy disconnect goes back to the proxy on that machine.

    What it guarantees. A token is never taken from the command line, and never written into config.yaml as such (only ${secret:...} or ${env:...} references). Plain http:// to another machine is refused unless you pass --allow-insecure.

    What is not verified #

    The setups on this page were run on one Linux machine: several proxies on loopback with stand-in model services, a stand-in identity provider through a TLS front, and the replicas with a local PostgreSQL and a stand-in Redis. Not yet run: real Google, Microsoft Entra ID, Okta or GitHub accounts, chains over HTTPS between machines, real GitHub Copilot accounts behind a hub, a real Kubernetes cluster, a real Redis, and macOS or Windows. The CI recipes were run as shell commands on Linux, with a stand-in model service and a stand-in for a service container; not on GitHub's own runners.
