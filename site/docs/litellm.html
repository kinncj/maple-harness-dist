    LiteLLM is a gateway many teams already run in front of their model accounts. The Maple proxy works with it in both directions: it can use a LiteLLM gateway as one of its providers, and a LiteLLM gateway can use the Maple proxy as one of its models.

    The short version

    In plain words #

    • What it is. Two ways to combine them. Maple in front: your tools talk to Maple, and Maple sends some models' requests to your team's LiteLLM. LiteLLM in front: your team's LiteLLM sends some models' requests to a Maple proxy, which serves them from its own accounts (a GitHub Copilot seat, for example).
    • Why you want it. Keep the gateway your team already has, and add what Maple does: Copilot accounts and Copilot Fusion, fallbacks between accounts, its budgets, and its usage log.
    • What to do. For Maple in front, add a provider of type litellm. For LiteLLM in front, add a model in LiteLLM's config.yaml whose api_base is the Maple proxy's address. Moving over for good? Import the LiteLLM config, or a running LiteLLM, with maple-proxy import litellm.

    Pick your way round:

    • Maple in front of LiteLLM Your tools talk to Maple; some models come from your team's LiteLLM.
    • LiteLLM in front of Maple Your LiteLLM serves some models from a Maple proxy's accounts.
    • Moving a client from LiteLLM to Maple A tool set up for LiteLLM needs only a new address and token.
    • LiteLLM's management API Scripts written for LiteLLM's /key, /user and /team keep working.
    • Move a LiteLLM config to Maple Translate a LiteLLM config, or a running LiteLLM, into Maple settings.

    What was tested

    Tested with LiteLLM 1.110.0 on 2026-11-18: chat completions (streamed and not), the Responses API, Anthropic messages through LiteLLM, and tool calls, including Codex CLI's whole tool loop through Maple and LiteLLM. Other versions may differ.

    Run again on 2026-11-21 against LiteLLM 1.110.0 (installed from PyPI, with its PostgreSQL database), both ways, with a stand-in model server: also the cost header in both directions, telling LiteLLM who asked (seen in LiteLLM's own spend logs), a LiteLLM token naming your people, LiteLLM's tag and key headers, LiteLLM's own Python client against the management API, the live import, serving its live model list (models added to LiteLLM later included), its session and end-user headers sent to Maple, and a LiteLLM serving https with a certificate from a private CA (trusted through SSL_CERT_FILE). LiteLLM keeps a caller's x-litellm-session-id for itself: it does not reach Maple.

    Maple in front of LiteLLM #

    Add your LiteLLM gateway as a provider, with a LiteLLM key, and list the models you want from it:

    YAML
    providers:
  - id: team-litellm
    type: litellm
    base_url: https://litellm.example.com/v1   # your gateway, with /v1
    api_key: ${secret:team-litellm}           # a LiteLLM key
    models:
      - id: gpt-team                           # a model name LiteLLM serves
        supports_tools: true

    On the setup page, Providers offers LiteLLM; Detect models lists what the key may use and fills in each model's context window from LiteLLM's model information. A key that may not read that information still lists its models, without the windows.

    Each model is then served like any other: gpt-team or team-litellm/gpt-team, with aliases, fallbacks, budgets and the usage log. Its requests show team-litellm in the usage log's provider column, and the response header X-Maple-Served-By: team-litellm/gpt-team.

    What Maple sees of the cost. LiteLLM says what each request cost, and Maple records that figure as reported by LiteLLM: the usage log's list_cost_usd holds it, with price_list_version litellm and price_basis reported, so it is never mistaken for Maple's own estimate, and dollar budgets count it. A request LiteLLM gave no figure for has none. A price set on the model still gives Maple's cost preview and savings a figure of your own, beside LiteLLM's.

    • The figure is LiteLLM's x-litellm-response-cost response header, read for chat completions (streamed or not) and for requests passed on to LiteLLM as they came (the other OpenAI routes and pass-through routes), over https or to this machine only: over plain http to another machine, anyone on the way could change it, so it is not read. A value that is not a plain positive number of dollars, or above $500 for one request, is not recorded.
    • LiteLLM computes it from its own price table, so it is what LiteLLM would charge, not your provider's bill.

    Telling LiteLLM who asked #

    In plain words. By default LiteLLM sees every request from Maple as coming from Maple's key. If you want LiteLLM's own spend records and budgets to tell your people and teams apart, say so, and Maple sends their ids along. Nothing else about the caller is sent. Set it below, in config.yaml, or on the setup page (Providers, Model details and cloud accounts), which also sets the live model list of the next section.

    YAML
    providers:
  - id: team-litellm
    type: litellm
    base_url: https://litellm.example.com/v1
    api_key: ${secret:team-litellm}
    litellm:
      forward: [person, team]      # either or both; leave it out to send nothing

    The technical view.

    • person sends the person's id as the OpenAI user field (LiteLLM's end user) and as the tag maple-person:<id> in metadata.tags; team sends the tag maple-team:<id>. These are the ids in your people: and teams:; an id with characters other than letters, digits and . _ - @ : is not sent.
    • Chat completions carry them in the body. A request passed on to LiteLLM as it came carries them in LiteLLM's headers instead, x-litellm-end-user-id and x-litellm-tags (replacing any the caller set), since its body is the caller's own. A request with no person (no people set up) sends nothing.
    • No token, name, address or other request detail is ever sent this way.

    Serving the models LiteLLM lists #

    In plain words. Instead of writing each model under the provider, Maple can serve whatever your LiteLLM lists, and pick up models added to it later, within a list of names you allow.

    YAML
        litellm:
      live_models:
        allow: ["gpt-*", "claude-*"]   # required: names or patterns; "*" for all
        refresh_minutes: 10            # how often the list is read again (the default)

    The technical view.

    • The list is LiteLLM's /v1/models for this key, read when the proxy starts and every refresh_minutes; each model's window and longest reply come from /v1/model/info when the key may read it. If LiteLLM cannot be reached, the last list stays and it is asked again within 15 seconds (so a proxy that starts before LiteLLM serves its models soon after LiteLLM is up).
    • A listed model is served by its LiteLLM name, or as team-litellm/<name> when another provider already has that name: the models you configure always come first. A model LiteLLM stops listing stops being served.
    • allow decides which listed names are served at all; your people's and teams' model lists still apply on top.

    Moving a client from LiteLLM to Maple #

    A tool set up for a LiteLLM gateway sends the gateway's key in the x-litellm-api-key header (for Claude Code, through ANTHROPIC_CUSTOM_HEADERS). The Maple proxy reads its access token from that header too, ahead of Authorization, so such a tool needs only its address changed and a Maple access token in place of the LiteLLM key. On this machine the proxy needs no token unless people are set up; a token sent there in x-litellm-api-key names the person, and a wrong one is refused, as in Authorization.

    The other things LiteLLM's clients send are read too:

    LiteLLMWhat Maple does with it
    x-litellm-tags header, or metadata.tags / a top-level tags list in a chat requestThe request's tags (the usage log's tags column, routing by tags, and the priority: tag), when X-Maple-Tags is absent
    x-litellm-session-idThe request's session, when X-Maple-Session is absent
    x-litellm-end-user-id, x-litellm-customer-idThe end user, for end-user budgets, when you set no end_users.header of your own, before the body's user
    userThe end user; or the person, from a token allowed to name people (see LiteLLM in front of Maple)
    metadata (other fields)Not sent on to any provider

    Errors keep OpenAI's shape (error.message, type, code), which LiteLLM's clients and the OpenAI SDKs read; code holds Maple's reason (budget_exceeded, context_length_exceeded, rate_limited, ...), where LiteLLM's model routes put the HTTP status. The management API below answers in LiteLLM's own shape, code holding the status.

    LiteLLM's management API #

    Scripts and tools that manage a LiteLLM gateway's keys, users and teams (LiteLLM's /key, /user and /team routes, or its litellm-proxy command) can manage a Maple proxy the same way, with its address changed. The routes are on the proxy's OpenAI-compatible address, and need an access token with the manage scope, in place of LiteLLM's master key:

    Shell
    maple-proxy token add platform-admin --scope manage     # shown once; keep it like a password
export LITELLM_PROXY_URL=http://127.0.0.1:11535 LITELLM_PROXY_API_KEY=<that token>
litellm-proxy users create --email alice@example.com --team platform --max-budget 20
litellm-proxy keys generate --user-id alice@example.com --key-alias alice-laptop

    A manage token can make and revoke tokens and change people, teams and budgets, so it is never made through a pairing code or single sign-on, and it is needed on this machine too. It sends no model requests.

    With management_api: on, people's own tokens reach these routes too, as far as their role goes: a viewer reads everything, an org_admin looks after its organization's teams and their people, a team_admin its team's members, a member itself and its own keys. See roles.

    How LiteLLM's words map to Maple's:

    LiteLLMMaple
    a keyan access token with the proxy scope; key_alias is its label
    a user (user_id, or user_email when no id is given)a person
    a teama team; a person belongs to one team
    user_roleproxy_admin for an admin, proxy_admin_viewer for a viewer, org_admin for an org_admin, internal_user for everyone else; a team_admin is internal_user with the admin role in the team's members_with_roles, as LiteLLM names a team admin
    modelsthe person's or team's allowed models
    max_budget with budget_duration 1d or 30da daily or monthly dollar budget (other periods are refused)
    rpm_limit, max_parallel_requestsrequests a minute, requests at once
    tpm_limitestimated input tokens a minute (LiteLLM counts all tokens)
    durationthe token's expiry
    /key/block, /key/unblock, /key/regenerate with grace_periodblock, unblock, rotate (at most 7 days' grace)
    • A key made without a user gets a person of its own (key-<alias>-<id>), which holds the models, budget and limits given with it. A key made for a user shares that user's, so models, budgets and limits for it are set on the user (/user/update); a key-level value there is refused.
    • Deleting a user revokes their keys; their remote-control devices and other tokens are only untied.
    • Fields Maple does not act on (metadata, guardrails, and others) are named in the answer's X-Maple-Ignored header, never dropped silently.
    • The secret is made by Maple. A request that sets key itself is refused, so litellm-proxy keys import does not carry LiteLLM's keys over: make new ones.
    • /key/info?key=<secret> puts a secret in the address. Maple never logs it, but a reverse proxy in front of it may: ask with the key's id instead.
    • /model/info lists every model with the provider behind it, so it needs the manage token too (or, with roles on, an admin's or a viewer's); people's keys list their models with /v1/models.
    • /team/member_delete takes a person out of the team named only if they are in it.
    • spend on a user, team or key is the person's or team's estimated dollars this month, at list prices, through this proxy. Dollars are counted only once some dollar budget is set; until then spend is null, not 0.
    • /health (manage token, or an admin's or viewer's) lists the models as healthy or unhealthy endpoints. LiteLLM sends a test request to each model; Maple answers from how real requests fared lately, so it costs nothing and a model nobody has used yet counts as healthy.
    • Probes. Where the proxy answers probes (in a container, in central mode, or with MAPLE_PROBES=on; see Docker), LiteLLM's /health/liveliness (and /health/liveness) and /health/readiness answer like /healthz and /readyz, in LiteLLM's shape and without a token, so manifests written for LiteLLM keep working.

    Tested with LiteLLM's litellm-proxy 1.110.0 on 2026-11-18: users (create, get, list, delete), keys (generate, list, delete) and teams (list) against a Maple proxy, and the key it made sending a model request. On 2026-11-21 LiteLLM 1.110.0's own Python client made the same calls on a real LiteLLM and on a Maple proxy (users, teams and their members, keys made, read, listed, changed and deleted, model info, readiness) and the answers were compared: every call that worked on LiteLLM worked on Maple, with the fields LiteLLM's CLI reads, except where this page says Maple differs. Two more differences it showed: /user/new makes no key (LiteLLM's makes one; make it with /key/generate), and /models lists models for a token with the proxy scope (a manage token reads them at /model/info). LiteLLM's admin page, its spend routes and other routes are not served.

    LiteLLM in front of Maple #

    In LiteLLM's config.yaml, add a model whose api_base is the Maple proxy. Use LiteLLM's openai/ prefix with the name the Maple proxy serves:

    YAML
    model_list:
  - model_name: copilot-fusion-high
    litellm_params:
      model: openai/github-copilot/fusion-high   # a name the Maple proxy lists
      api_base: https://maple.example.com:11535/v1
      api_key: os.environ/MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN      # a Maple access token

    LiteLLM's callers then use copilot-fusion-high through LiteLLM's chat completions, Responses API or Anthropic messages endpoint, and the Maple proxy answers from its own accounts, with its fallbacks and budgets.

    • A token for LiteLLM. A Maple proxy on another machine needs an access token for each caller (Using it from another machine). Give LiteLLM its own token, or one per LiteLLM team, so Maple's usage log and budgets can tell them apart: everything LiteLLM sends with one token counts as one caller in Maple.
    • Your people behind LiteLLM. If LiteLLM's callers are your people, let LiteLLM's token name the person each request is for, and their own models, budgets and limits apply (A gateway that names people):
      YAML
      keys:
  - token: <the token LiteLLM uses>
    names_people: [alice, bob, team:platform]   # whom it may name; no wildcard

      The OpenAI user field LiteLLM sends then names the person (by their id in people:); anyone else is refused with 403 person_not_found (through LiteLLM: a 403 with Maple's reason, in LiteLLM's error shape). Without names_people, user only names an end user, as before. LiteLLM passes on what its callers send unless it sets user itself, so use this only where LiteLLM sets user from the caller it signed in (see the link above).

    • LiteLLM sends user on only when told. LiteLLM 1.110.0 sends the user field to OpenAI's own model names only, so for a Maple model add allowed_openai_params: [user] to its litellm_params; without it Maple never sees who the request is for:
      YAML
          litellm_params:
      model: openai/copilot-fusion-high
      api_base: https://maple.example.com:11535/v1
      api_key: os.environ/MAPLE_PROXY_TOKEN
      allowed_openai_params: [user]
    • Maple's cost, in LiteLLM. A Maple answer that is not streamed carries x-litellm-response-cost, Maple's dollar figure for it (the same one its dollar budgets count; 0 for an answer from the response cache), and LiteLLM 1.110.0 records that as the request's cost unless the model has prices of its own in LiteLLM's config. An answer with no dollar figure (a GitHub Copilot account counts AI units) carries none, and LiteLLM prices it its own way. A streamed answer carries none either: the cost is known only once it ends.
    • Callers on LiteLLM's Anthropic endpoint. LiteLLM translates its /v1/messages requests for an openai/ model, so they reach Maple's OpenAI-compatible address too; that was part of the test above, and so was LiteLLM's anthropic/ prefix pointed at Maple's Anthropic-compatible address.

    Running both while you move. You do not have to switch at once: put Maple in front and give it your LiteLLM as a provider (above), so models you have not moved yet still go through LiteLLM; or keep LiteLLM in front and add Maple as one of its models. Move one model or one team at a time, and compare the usage log with LiteLLM's spend records as you go.

    A whole config, side by side #

    In plain words. Here is a typical LiteLLM config.yaml and the Maple settings maple-proxy import litellm makes from it, unchanged, followed by what came across differently. Your own file's report says the same for yours.

    The LiteLLM config:

    YAML
    model_list:
  - model_name: gpt-4o
    litellm_params:
      model: openai/gpt-4o
      api_key: os.environ/OPENAI_API_KEY
      rpm: 500
  - model_name: gpt-4o
    litellm_params:
      model: azure/gpt-4o-eu
      api_base: https://contoso-eu.openai.azure.com
      api_key: os.environ/AZURE_EU_KEY
      api_version: "2024-10-21"
      rpm: 300
  - model_name: claude-sonnet
    litellm_params:
      model: anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-5
      api_key: os.environ/ANTHROPIC_API_KEY
      max_parallel_requests: 8
  - model_name: claude-opus
    litellm_params:
      model: anthropic/claude-opus-4-1
      api_key: os.environ/ANTHROPIC_API_KEY
      max_parallel_requests: 2

router_settings:
  routing_strategy: simple-shuffle
  num_retries: 2
  timeout: 120
  fallbacks:
    - gpt-4o: ["claude-sonnet"]
  model_group_alias:
    smart: claude-opus

litellm_settings:
  context_window_fallbacks:
    - claude-sonnet: ["claude-opus"]
  cache: true
  cache_params:
    type: redis
    ttl: 600
  success_callback: ["langfuse"]

general_settings:
  master_key: os.environ/LITELLM_MASTER_KEY
  database_url: os.environ/DATABASE_URL

guardrails:
  - guardrail_name: pii-mask
    litellm_params:
      guardrail: presidio
      mode: pre_call
      default_on: true

    What the importer prints for it (maple-proxy import litellm litellm-config.yaml; the keys stay in the environment variables LiteLLM read them from):

    YAML
    providers:
    - id: openai
      type: openai-compatible
      base_url: https://api.openai.com/v1
      api_key: ${env:OPENAI_API_KEY}
      models:
        - id: gpt-4o
          supports_tools: true
          limits:
            requests_per_minute: 500
      first_output_timeout_seconds: 120
    - id: azure-contoso-eu
      type: azure
      base_url: https://contoso-eu.openai.azure.com
      api_key: ${env:AZURE_EU_KEY}
      models:
        - id: gpt-4o-eu
          supports_tools: true
          limits:
            requests_per_minute: 300
      azure:
        api_version: "2024-10-21"
      first_output_timeout_seconds: 120
    - id: anthropic
      type: anthropic
      api_key: ${env:ANTHROPIC_API_KEY}
      models:
        - id: claude-sonnet-4-5
          supports_tools: true
          limits:
            max_concurrent: 8
        - id: claude-opus-4-1
          supports_tools: true
          limits:
            max_concurrent: 2
      first_output_timeout_seconds: 120
model_aliases:
    claude-opus: anthropic/claude-opus-4-1
    claude-sonnet: anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-5
    smart: anthropic/claude-opus-4-1
model_groups:
    - name: gpt-4o
      strategy: weighted
      deployments:
        - model: openai/gpt-4o

        - model: azure-contoso-eu/gpt-4o-eu
          weight: 300
      retries: 2

    claude-sonnet:
        - claude-opus
    gpt-4o:
        - claude-sonnet
response_cache: "on"
response_cache_ttl: 600s
retries: 2
guardrails:
    - name: pii-mask
      type: pii
      action: mask
      phases:
        - pre_call
      default_on: true

    What is different.

    • Accounts, not deployments. Each address and key becomes one provider (openai, azure-contoso-eu, anthropic); the two gpt-4o deployments become a model group, weighted 500 to 300 by their rpm, as LiteLLM's simple-shuffle weights them.
    • Names. claude-sonnet, claude-opus and LiteLLM's smart alias become model aliases of the Anthropic models.
    • Timeout. timeout: 120 limits how long a provider may take to start answering (first_output_timeout_seconds); once it answers, a reply may stream for as long as it needs.
    • Retries and fallbacks. num_retries: 2 is retries: 2 for every model, and for the gpt-4o group's own, never once a provider has taken the request; the context_window_fallbacks join the ordinary fallbacks, tried when a model fails, a request too long for it included.
    • Limits. rpm and max_parallel_requests become each model's own limits on its account (limits.requests_per_minute, limits.max_concurrent); a request over one goes to the model's fallbacks, as LiteLLM routes around a deployment at its limit.
    • Cache. The response cache answers a repeat only when the request allows it (X-Maple-Cache: allow), and is not kept in LiteLLM's Redis.
    • Guardrail. The Presidio guardrail becomes Maple's own PII check, which masks what it finds on this machine.
    • Not carried over. The langfuse callback (set Maple's own up with callbacks: and its keys), database_url (Maple keeps its data in its config folder; several proxies share one with storage.mode: central), the master key (make a token with --scope manage), and LiteLLM's virtual keys (make a token per person or tool).

    Move a LiteLLM config to Maple #

    In plain words. maple-proxy import litellm reads your LiteLLM config.yaml and writes the matching Maple settings: your models, their accounts, groups of deployments, fallbacks and MCP servers. It prints what it would add and a report of what came across, what came across a little differently, and what has no equivalent, with what to do instead. Nothing changes until you ask it to.

    1. Read the report first. Run the import without --write: it prints the Maple settings it would add, and the report of what came across, what came across a little differently, and what has no equivalent.
    2. Settle the keys. A key written in the LiteLLM file is never copied into config.yaml: add --store-keys to keep those keys in the secret store, or change them to os.environ/NAME in the LiteLLM file and import again.
    3. Write it. Run it again with --write, which merges the result into the proxy's config.yaml, keeping its comments and other sections.
    4. Set the environment variables the report names, then start the proxy: it does not start while a new provider's key variable is not set.
    5. Make new tokens for your people (maple-proxy token add <label> --person <id>): LiteLLM's virtual keys do not come across.
    Shell
    maple-proxy import litellm litellm-config.yaml              # print the Maple settings and the report; change nothing
maple-proxy import litellm litellm-config.yaml --write      # merge them into the proxy's config.yaml
maple-proxy import litellm litellm-config.yaml --json       # the report as JSON, for a script

    The technical view.

    • The translated settings go to standard output and the report to standard error. With --json, one JSON object goes to standard output instead: the translated settings (config_yaml), every report item with its status, the counts, the files include: brought in, and where keys are written in the file (never the keys); with --write, it comes after the merge and names the file written, and the messages go to standard error.
    • include: files are read as LiteLLM reads them: their lists added to the config's, their other values in place of the config's, each file once, a file's own include: relative to it. Only files inside the LiteLLM config's folder are read (not through .. or a symbolic link out of it), only regular files, at most 32 of them; a file that cannot be read stops the import, changing nothing. --write merges them into config.yaml (the one --config names, or the default one), keeping its comments and other sections. It refuses, changing nothing, when a provider, model name, fallback or MCP server it adds already exists; --replace replaces those instead.
    • Keys. os.environ/NAME becomes ${env:NAME}, so the proxy reads the same variable. A key written in the LiteLLM file is never printed or copied into config.yaml: the config refers to the secret store (${secret:provider/<id>/api_key}), and --write refuses until you choose: --store-keys keeps those keys in the secret store (the keyring when there is one), checking each reads back before the config changes; or change them to os.environ/NAME in the LiteLLM file and import again. An address with a query (?key=...) is not copied either, since it may hold a key, and an MCP server value written in Maple's own ${...} form is refused.
    • Models. Each model_list entry becomes a provider (one per address and key, so deployments on one account share it) and a model. A model_name different from the model's own name becomes an alias; several deployments under one model_name become a model group. model_info limits and prices are kept.
    • Routing. simple-shuffle becomes weighted (by weight, or by rpm), or round-robin without weights; least-busy and latency-based-routing map directly; deployments with order become ordered. num_retries becomes the top-level retries (at most 3), and each model group's own. fallbacks, content_policy_fallbacks and model_group_alias are kept; default_fallbacks is written out for each model, and context_window_fallbacks are added to the model's fallbacks: Maple tries them when the model fails, a request too long for it included, and passes over one whose context window is too small. A timeout becomes first_output_timeout_seconds, which limits how long a provider may take to start answering, not the whole reply.
    • Limits. A deployment's rpm, tpm and max_parallel_requests become the model's own limits on its account (limits.requests_per_minute, limits.input_tokens_per_minute, limits.max_concurrent): over one, the account is passed over and the model's fallbacks tried. Maple counts the input tokens it estimates before sending, where LiteLLM's tpm counts all tokens. rpm also still weights a model group's deployments.
    • Guardrails become Maple guardrails where Maple has the same check: LiteLLM's generic guardrail API (a webhook guardrail), OpenAI moderation, Bedrock Guardrails (with the imported bedrock provider), Azure Content Safety and Prompt Shields, Google Model Armor, and Presidio and hide-secrets as Maple's own PII and secret checks. mode becomes the phases (during_call runs before sending), logging_only the log_only action, and default_on is kept as LiteLLM means it: off unless set, so such a guardrail runs only for a request that names it (in X-Maple-Guardrails). A guardrail's key comes from a MAPLE_GUARDRAIL_... variable the report names, or the secret store; with a key, its address is kept beside it. Other guardrails are reported.
    • MCP servers in mcp_servers become MCP hub servers, added switched off; turn each on with maple-proxy mcp-hub enable <name>. As with maple-proxy mcp-hub add, writing a server that runs a command, or a header that refers to a secret, needs you at a terminal.
    • Not carried over, with a line in the report each: LiteLLM's virtual keys (LiteLLM keeps only their hashes: make new tokens with maple-proxy token add <label> [--person <id>], and tools keep working with the Maple address and that token, in Authorization or x-litellm-api-key), the master_key (make a manage token instead, above), agents, callbacks (Maple has its own, set up with their keys), guardrails Maple has no check for, cooldown settings, and wildcard models.
    • Providers Maple does not speak. A deployment with a provider Maple has no equivalent for is reported. With --via-litellm <address>, such deployments are served through your existing LiteLLM instead, by their LiteLLM names, with a LiteLLM key from LITELLM_API_KEY; a provider Maple does not know that has an api_base is otherwise assumed to speak the OpenAI API.
    • Read the report before you start the proxy with the result: it lists the environment variables the new providers read their keys from, and the proxy does not start while one of them is not set.

    Note

    Tested on sample LiteLLM configs (by the proxy's own config checks); not yet against a large real deployment.

    From a running LiteLLM #

    In plain words. If your LiteLLM keeps models, teams and users in its database (added on its admin page or through its API), they are not in config.yaml. --from-url asks the running LiteLLM for them instead, and brings its teams and users across as Maple people and teams, with their budgets and limits. It only reads; it never changes anything in LiteLLM.

    Shell
    export LITELLM_MASTER_KEY=...                                             # LiteLLM's admin key, typed into the environment
maple-proxy import litellm --from-url https://litellm.example.com           # print the result and the report; change nothing
maple-proxy import litellm --from-url https://litellm.example.com --write   # merge it into config.yaml

    The technical view.

    • The key. LiteLLM's admin key (its master key, or a key with the proxy_admin role) is read from LITELLM_MASTER_KEY, or from the variable --key-env NAME names. There is no flag that takes the key itself, so it does not land in your shell history. It is sent only to the address you give: a redirect to another address is not followed. A refused key is reported as such, without the key or LiteLLM's answer.
    • The address. https:// is required, except to this machine (http://127.0.0.1:4000 or localhost). An address with a user name, a password or a query is refused.
    • An HTTP proxy. The proxy set in the environment (HTTPS_PROXY) is not used unless you add --use-proxy-env (https only; NO_PROXY is honoured). With it, LiteLLM's own address is still checked first, as for a direct connection, and the encrypted session is with LiteLLM itself, so the proxy in between sees neither the key nor the answers.
    • What is read. GET /model/info (the deployments), the team list (/v2/team/list, or /team/list on older versions), /user/list, /key/list (each key's alias, owner and limits only), every page, and /router/settings (the routing strategy, retries, timeout, fallbacks and model group aliases; nothing else of that answer is kept).
    • Models come across as from a file's model_list, above. LiteLLM's API does not give out the deployments' keys, so none is copied: a provider whose key LiteLLM read from os.environ/NAME reads ${env:NAME}; any other reads its provider's usual variable (OPENAI_API_KEY, for example), and the report names each one. Deployments on one address are joined into one provider, as LiteLLM does not show whether their keys differ: split them by hand if they do. The report says which variable each provider sends, and to which address: check them, since they come from LiteLLM's answer. A deployment whose key would be the admin key's variable is left out, and an address LiteLLM gives as os.environ/NAME is not read from your environment (add those deployments by hand).
    • Teams become teams, named after the team's alias (made into a usable id when it is not one), and users become people, named after their email when they have one, in their first team. max_budget over a budget_duration of a day or a month becomes a daily or monthly dollar budget (Maple counts calendar days and months); over a whole number of days up to 62, such as a week, a budget window of that many days (what was spent over the last 7 days, say: at least as strict as LiteLLM's week from its last reset); over another period, its share of a day (estimated, and stricter). rpm_limit, tpm_limit (counted by Maple as input tokens) and max_parallel_requests become the person's or team's limits, and models the allow-list.
    • Nothing comes across looser than it was. A team or user that cannot be carried over as it is, or stricter, is left out, with a line in the report saying why: a budget that resets more often than daily, no-default-models, a blocked team, and a user whose team was left out. A budget that never resets becomes a lifetime budget, counting what is spent through Maple from the import (LiteLLM's recorded spend is not carried over).
    • Not carried over, each with a line in the report: virtual keys (make a token for each person with maple-proxy token add <label> --person <id>; for a key with rpm_limit, tpm_limit or max_parallel_requests the report gives the keys[].limits to add for its new token, and for a key with a max_budget of its own the keys[].budget, read from /key/list, which never gives out the key), LiteLLM's default_user_id (its stand-in for the master key, not a person), recorded spend, team admins, and what LiteLLM keeps only in its config.yaml (litellm_settings: the cache, callbacks, default fallbacks; and the routing of a LiteLLM without /router/settings): import that file as well.
    • --write merges as with a file. A person or team that already exists is a clash; with --replace, their team, budget, limits and models are replaced and their tokens kept, unless that would raise or remove a budget or limit, or allow another model: then it is refused, and you change it by hand.

    Tested against a stand-in that answers as LiteLLM 1.110.0's management API does, and on 2026-11-21 against a real LiteLLM 1.110.0 with its database (a team, a user and a key made there, and its router settings); --use-proxy-env not through a real company proxy.
